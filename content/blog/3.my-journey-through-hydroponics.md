# My Journey Through Hydroponics

It's been a while since I wanted to start growing my own greens using hydroponics, and I finally started! I will use this article to share my experiences planning and setting up my first hydroponic system.

## The requirements

- Growing lettuce
- Deep Water Culture (DWC)
- Fully indoor
- Food grade materials only (no PVC!)
- As cheap and simple as possible
- Low maintenance requirement
- Home assistant integration 😇

That's it, now let's choose the parts.

## The parts

Keep in mind that everything needs to be 100% food safe, you don't want nasty chemicals in your lettuce, don't you?

So stick with Polypropylene (PP) or other food safe plastics.

Also avoid anything translucent to prevent any algae growth inside your container, or they'll eat all your nutrients and leave nothing to your lettuce.

- 4x IKEA UPPSNOFSAD boxes with lids
- 2x geranium flower boxes as bottom trays
- 2x ice cube boxes as seed trays
- 24x net cups (I've used 55x50mm conical ones)
- A bag of clay pebbles
- Nutrient solution, an organic one is best
- White LEDs at least 5000K for green leaves
- A low power fan (maybe two)

## Building the light

Lettuce wants a lot of light, but I didn't want to drop a hundred euros on a "real" grow light. So I went DIY: five 5000K white LED strips glued onto an aluminium sheet that doubles as a heatsink and a cheap reflector.

![LED strips glued to an aluminium panel](/images/hydroponics-led-strips.jpg)

The panel hangs from a small wooden frame that sits over the growing area. Nothing fancy — four pieces of wood, a few screws, and enough clearance to slide trays in and out.

![Wooden frame holding the LED panel above the workbench](/images/hydroponics-light-frame.jpg)

## Germination

Before the DWC system was even drilled, I started the seeds in plastic ice cube boxes filled with a cheap organic seed-starting mix, sitting inside geranium flower boxes that catch the runoff. Cover, light, and a little patience.

![Net cups, ice cube boxes used as seed trays, and a fan staged on the workbench](/images/hydroponics-parts-on-bench.jpg)

To keep things warm and bounce the LEDs back onto the seedlings, I wrapped the whole shelf in a sheet of mylar emergency blanket. It looks ridiculous, but it works.

![Soap-box seed trays under the LED panel surrounded by reflective foil](/images/hydroponics-germination-setup.jpg)

The reflective wrap also turns the shelf into a tiny greenhouse. The seedlings stayed around 16-17°C with humidity pinned at almost 100% — perfect for lettuce.

![Thermometer reading 16.5°C and 99% humidity next to a seed tray](/images/hydroponics-thermometer.jpg)

Sprouting happened on schedule. First shy ones on day 3, then the rest piled in over the following days.

## Drilling the lids

Each UPPSNOFSAD lid gets six 50mm holes for the net cups. I used a hole saw on low speed — PP plastic melts if you push too fast — and made sure to deburr the edges so the net cups would sit flush.

![Drilling a 50mm hole in the IKEA box lid with a hole saw](/images/hydroponics-drilling-lid.jpg)

Four boxes, six holes each, twenty-four plants. The lids snap right back onto the boxes and the net cups drop in cleanly.

![Four IKEA boxes with their lids drilled and net cups installed](/images/hydroponics-dwc-lids-ready.jpg)

## Transplanting

Transplant day is the satisfying part. Each seedling gets gently teased out of its soil cell and nested into a net cup full of pre-soaked clay pebbles. The pebbles hold the plant in place while letting the roots grow down into the nutrient solution.

![Close-up of a lettuce seedling nested in clay pebbles inside a net cup](/images/hydroponics-transplant-closeup.jpg)

I filled each box with tap water plus a few drops of the organic nutrient solution, set the lids on top, and slid them under the LED panel.

![Four DWC boxes installed under the LED panel with seedlings in place](/images/hydroponics-system-installed.jpg)

## Growing

The first two weeks after transplanting are the slowest. The plants are figuring out the new environment — soil roots die, water roots grow, and the leaves barely change.

Then everything happens at once. Once the roots reach the nutrient solution, the lettuce explodes. From "tiny green specks" to "actual lettuce" in roughly three weeks.

## What I'd change next time

A few things I learned the hard way:

- **Start fewer seeds.** I sowed two or three per cell "just in case" and ended up culling perfectly healthy seedlings. Lettuce germinates at >90% — one seed per cell is plenty.
- **More light.** Five LED strips look bright to the eye, but lettuce really wants more. The plants grew, just slower and a bit leggier than they should have. Next build gets either denser strips or a second panel.
- **Add airflow from day one.** The first batch of seedlings came out floppy and fragile — no breeze means no stem strength. I bolted a spare PC fan onto the frame later and the next leaves came in noticeably tougher. Don't wait, put the fan in on the same day you turn the light on.
- **Watch the water level.** With no pump in a DWC, the only thing keeping the roots happy is the gap of air between the lid and the water. Top up before it shrinks.
- **The Home Assistant integration is still on the TODO list.** A simple ESP with a temperature/humidity sensor and a TDS probe would close the loop. For now, I check it manually — and that's fine.

Would I do it again? Already planning the next batch. 🥬
